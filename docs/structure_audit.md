# Structure audit: is each task really the type of data we assumed?

Generated by `python scripts/run_audit.py --out results/v2/audit.json --doc docs/structure_audit.md`. Raw values are in [`results/v2/audit.json`](../results/v2/audit.json).

The audit runs **before** any KAN or MLP is trained and uses no neural network. It asks four model-free questions:

1. **Linear or not.** How much does an additive spline model (a GAM) beat ridge regression? That gain is the *nonlinearity* score.
2. **Interaction.** How much does a flexible model beat the additive one? The flexible model is the better of gradient boosting and RBF kernel ridge, and the gain is the *interaction* score. Signal of this kind needs inputs to be combined, so per-input curves cannot capture it.
3. **Shape of each input.** For each input, a smoothed 1-D fit is classified by its prominent turning points (hysteresis of 15 % of the fitted range):
   - *monotone*: no turning point;
   - *single-extremum*: one turning point, a bump or a U;
   - *oscillatory*: two or more.

   Inputs whose 1-D fit explains less than 10 % of the variance are labelled *flat* and get no shape claim. For monotone inputs, the gain of a 4-parameter sigmoid over a straight line measures *threshold/saturation*.
4. **Rhythm** (time-course tasks only). A cosinor F-test asks whether a 24 h cosine adds to a linear trend, per transcript, with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. In the blood data, subject means are removed first.

All R² values are 5-fold cross-validated, and grouped by subject for `circadian_blood`. Classification tasks use McFadden pseudo-R² of cross-validated probabilities. `drug_combo` values are means over the 12 replicate blocks. The `_fullrange` rows repeat the audit including the extrapolation region, to show where the structure lives.

## Positive controls behave as designed

The six synthetic targets show that the audit can tell the structures apart:
- `linear` gains nothing from flexibility.
- `additive` gains only from per-input curves: nonlinearity +0.41, interaction 0.
- `interaction` and `waves` are invisible to linear and additive models, which have R² ≈ 0. A flexible model reaches 0.99.
- `radial` shows single-extremum (bump) marginals on both inputs.
- `kink` shows a saturating monotone marginal plus interaction.

Product and periodic interactions leave flat marginals, because averaging over the other input cancels them. That is why the interaction score, not the marginal shape, is the evidence for those structures.

## What the biological data actually look like

| Task | Hypothesis | Verdict | Consequence for the KAN–MLP comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| `circadian_liver` | periodic | **Confirmed.** Linear R² ≈ 0 but additive R² 0.72, and all 10 transcripts are rhythmic (cosinor q < 0.05). Their relative amplitudes are 0.9–1.4 SD, and the 1-D fits show multiple turning points over 48 h | Clean periodic test, but only 24 training points |
| `circadian_blood` | periodic + trend | **Confirmed within subjects, weak across subjects.** All 10 transcripts are rhythmic after removing subject means (largest q = 6e-27). Yet pooled across people, the best flexible model reaches only R² 0.14: person-to-person baseline differences dominate. The ~27 h window covers about one cycle, so the time marginal shows a single extremum | Any model's achievable test R² for unseen subjects is low. Small differences between models are expected |
| `drug_combo` | sigmoid thresholds + interaction | **Mostly confirmed.** Interaction +0.29 ± 0.26 over 12 blocks. Drug A is monotone in 9/12 blocks. Drug B is monotone in only 6/12, and has a single extremum in the rest, consistent with a high-dose plateau or noise in 10-point dilution series. Block-to-block variation is large | Interaction and threshold test, at very small n (49 training wells per block) |
| `gb1_epistasis` | threshold global epistasis | **Not confirmed in the training region.** For pairs of non-deleterious mutations, double-mutant fitness is almost additive: linear R² 0.88, flexible 0.91. Interaction appears only when strongly deleterious singles are included (full range: +0.10), i.e. exactly in the held-out *outside* split | Inside test ≈ a near-linear task. The epistasis lives in the extrapolation region, so the outside split is the real test of the hypothesis |
| `mpsa_splicing` | high-order sequence interaction | **Confirmed.** Linear and additive R² 0.16, flexible 0.55 | Interaction on binary inputs: a KAN edge function on a ±1 input reduces to two values, so per-edge flexibility cannot be the source of any gain |
| `diabetes` | additive / linear | **Confirmed near-linear.** The flexible model does worse than ridge | Negative control |
| `breast_cancer` | linear-separable | **Confirmed.** Linear pseudo-R² 0.88 is the best of the three | Negative control |
| `yeast` | weak structure | **Confirmed near-linear and noisy.** Pseudo-R² ≈ 0.37 for all three models | Negative control |

**Bottom line.** Of the five biological tasks chosen for a specific structure:
- three show it: periodic liver, interacting drug surface, interacting splice sites;
- one shows it only within subjects: blood;
- one does not show it where models are trained: GB1.

The results report interprets each task by its **audited** structure, not by the original hypothesis.
