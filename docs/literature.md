# Spatially variable genes and evidence aggregation

This targeted review covers foundational detectors, major head-to-head comparisons and work directly relevant to evidence aggregation and uncertainty, through 19 November 2026. Literature discovery used searches for spatially variable gene detection, benchmark, ensemble, consensus, rank aggregation, calibration and instability, followed by publisher methods sections and linked code/data. This is a narrative review with targeted searches.

## The hypothesis determines what can be combined

| Target | Biological question | Representative methods and implications |
|---|---|---|
| Overall SVG | Does expression vary with spatial position across the section? | SpatialDE models spatial covariance with a GP. SPARK models counts using generalized spatial models and multiple kernels. SPARK-X tests expression–location dependence with kernels. nnSVG estimates gene-specific spatial scales using nearest-neighbor GPs. Moran's I and Geary's C measure graph autocorrelation at a chosen neighborhood scale. Spanve compares local and background expression differences. |
| Domain marker | Is a gene enriched in a specific region relative to a specified comparison? | SpaGCN uses detected domains and marker rules. DESpace tests expression differences across spatial clusters. Region annotation, comparator and learning the map from the same counts all affect interpretation. |
| Cell-type-specific SVG | Does expression vary spatially within a specified cell type or after modeling cell-type composition? | C-SIDE, CTSV and spVC address related conditional questions. Composition-driven signals can be true overall SVGs and null for a within-cell-type estimand. |

Gene rankings can also come from graph spectral scores (SpaGFT), diffusion (sepal), and spatial binarization (BinSpect). Such scores can enter rank aggregation directly. Any use as inferential evidence requires a valid score-to-p-value calibration. GP smoothness and graph scale affect sensitivity to gradients, sharp boundaries, disconnected patches and very local patterns. The same gene can rank differently under different scales and inputs. [Yan, Hua & Li, taxonomy of 34 methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56080-w), [Patel et al., benchmarking implementations](https://github.com/pinellolab/SVG_Benchmarking), [DESpace](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae027), [cell-type-specific benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag190).

## What benchmark papers establish

| Study | Evidence and implications |
|---|---|
| [Charitakis et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-03045-1) | Reported substantial discrepancies among callers and sensitivity to analytical choices. Agreement is an outcome worth reporting and an unreliable substitute for known truth. |
| [Chen, Kim & Yang (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-03145-y) | Compared rankings and selections on diverse spatial data alongside accuracy, robustness and computational considerations. Shared preprocessing, gene universe and matched list size matter for interpreting overlap. |
| [Benchmarking algorithms for SVG identification (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40139667/) | Independent evaluation and reusable framework. Its [code and archived release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15031083) provide a second starting point for method wrappers and dataset provenance. |
| [Patel et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03731-2) | Evaluated 14 methods across 96 datasets with ranking, classification, null calibration, scalability and downstream criteria including spatial ATAC. SPARK-X led overall ranking and Moran's I was competitive. Many p-values were miscalibrated. Reuse the controlled spatial signals and calibration comparisons, and treat clustering utility as a separate endpoint. |
| [Kang et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf303) | Compared domain maps and derived domain markers across platforms. Domain results depended on platform and caller. The method-derived real-data reference mixes an intersection and Moran's I recovery rule, making it a measure of concordance with specific callers. |
| [Large-scale SVG atlas (2025 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07653) | Evaluates cross-slide reproducibility, recovery of annotated markers and robustness at scale on human slides. Useful for external validation and checking what transfers across tissue types. Publication status and available artifacts should be recorded when pinning a release. |

## How published pipelines combine information

- **Within a caller.** SPARK and SPARK-X aggregate evidence across kernels for the same gene. This adds spatial-scale coverage within that model. Several related kernels or callers can still pick up the same nuisance pattern. [SPARK software and papers](https://github.com/xzhoulab/SPARK).
- **Across gene callers.** Castl (2026) is the direct comparator. Rank-Agg sums reciprocal ranks and selects a fixed top fraction. Pval-Agg combines p-values with a Cauchy transform and uses Benjamini–Yekutieli adjustment. FDPp-Agg augments the input with globally permuted expression, compares how often methods select real and artificial genes, and chooses a threshold using an FDP proxy. Its modules support different output types and treat reliance on individual callers as a practical problem. A paired comparison must match callers where possible, distinguish the official implementation from local approximations, and measure whether its controls capture technical spatial patterns for the dataset under test. [Castl paper and implementation availability](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag074).
- **Consensus sets and references.** Intersection, union and vote thresholds are natural comparators. Intersection rewards agreement and can miss a rare pattern captured by one caller. Unions reward sensitivity and need separate list calibration. Kang's majority vote aggregates **domain labels**, then tests markers. Its caller intersection serves a different role as a reference. Other evaluations have also used intersections, including MLN2SVG. [Kang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf303), [MLN2SVG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13137336/).
- **Across domain maps.** EnSDD weights member clusterings. STCC builds consensus clusterings. SpatialESD combines spot- and cluster-level relationships. These provide credible comparators for a domain-marker extension. InSTability estimates local sensitivity of domain labels to perturbation, motivating tests of whether boundary uncertainty reaches the derived gene list. [EnSDD](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-07001-y), [STCC](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/35/6/1415), [SpatialESD](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202520912), [InSTability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13692240/).
- **Post-clustering inference.** Count splitting separates discovery and testing under its count model. Negative-binomial splitting depends on the dispersion model. ClusterDE calibrates post-clustering DE with synthetic nulls. These address reuse of counts, while a domain-marker study must additionally define what qualifies as a spatial region and how it handles composition and boundaries. [Count splitting](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxac047), [NB splitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12985), [ClusterDE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13309996/).

## Remaining gap

The studies above motivate measuring gain from complementary callers **conditional on realistic nulls and on reproducibility across sections**. The specific gap to test is whether nuisance-matched joint calibration, explicit treatment of redundant caller families, and spatial perturbation summaries help beyond Castl and the strongest single caller. Raw concordance can stay high under shared depth artifacts. A fixed consensus can also suppress a true focal gene found consistently by one family. The benchmark must therefore distinguish true-null calibration, per-pattern sensitivity and cross-sample replication. The secondary gap is propagating domain-map instability into gene-level evidence while addressing count reuse. These are hypotheses derived from the reviewed studies.
